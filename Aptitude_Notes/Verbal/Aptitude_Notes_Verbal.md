# PART 1 — PARA SUMMARY BASICS

## 1.1 What is Reading Comprehension?

**Reading** = An activity (part of the process)  
**Comprehension** = Understanding

**What is measured?**
- Your ability to understand written English — overall understanding of the passage
- How well you can draw conclusions from information given
- Supporting ideas and facts given in the passage

## 1.2 Relevance in Tests

RC can **make or break** your score. It carries significant weight in aptitude tests.

## 1.3 Two Myths About RC

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| RC is a test of speed reading | RC tests comprehension, not speed |
| RC tests memorization of minute details | RC tests understanding of main ideas and inferences |

## 1.4 Is Vocabulary Important?

Vocabulary helps but is not the primary focus. Understanding context and main idea matters more.

## 1.5 RC Subject Areas

| Area | Topics |
|------|--------|
| **Social Sciences** | History, sociology, government |
| **Natural Sciences** | Biology, physics, chemistry, astronomy, botany |
| **Humanities** | Moral philosophy |
| **Business** | Economics, commerce |

# PART 2 — PARA SUMMARY TECHNIQUES

## 2.1 What is Para Summary?

Para Summary asks you to identify the **best summary** of a given paragraph. You must choose the option that captures the **main idea** without adding outside information or distorting the author's intent.

## 2.2 How to Solve Para Summary

1. **Read the paragraph carefully** — understand the main point
2. **Identify the author's intent** — what is the author trying to convey?
3. **Eliminate extreme options** — options with "always", "never", "only", "best" are often wrong
4. **Eliminate options that add new information** — not mentioned in the paragraph
5. **Eliminate options that distort meaning** — opposite of what author says
6. **Choose the most balanced, accurate option** — captures the essence without exaggeration

## 2.3 Common Wrong Option Types

| Type | Characteristics |
|------|-----------------|
| **Too narrow** | Focuses on a minor detail, misses main idea |
| **Too broad** | Generalizes beyond what the paragraph says |
| **Distorted** | Reverses or changes the author's meaning |
| **External info** | Brings in facts not in the paragraph |
| **Extreme language** | Uses "always", "never", "all", "only" |

## Passage 1 — Newspaper Advertisements

> However much we may complain about the number of advertisements there are in a newspaper, the fact remains that without advertisements there would be no newspapers. It's the advertisements that finance the newspapers and make it possible for us to buy them cheaply.

**Options:**
- A) The larger the circulation of a newspaper is, the greater is its need for good advertisements.
- B) Newspapers print so many advertisements that there is not much space left for the real news.
- C) It is general... (incomplete)

**Key Point:** Advertisements finance newspapers and keep them affordable. Despite complaints, ads are essential.

This five-mile-long fjord cuts straight into the middle of the island from its mouth, situated between the two towns of Northeast and Southwest Harbor. **Key Point:** Octopuses are marine animals with eight arms, found in all oceans, mostly on the ocean floor, some near the surface.

# PART 4 — PARA SUMMARY TEST — KEY PRINCIPLES

## 4.1 Do's

- ✅ Identify the **main idea**, not details
- ✅ Choose the option that **captures the essence**
- ✅ Stay **true to the author's tone**
- ✅ Eliminate clearly wrong options first

## 4.2 Don'ts

- ❌ Don't add outside knowledge
- ❌ Don't choose extreme statements
- ❌ Don't pick options that contradict the passage
- ❌ Don't focus on minor examples

## 4.3 Quick Elimination Strategy

1. Read all 4 options
2. Eliminate options with extreme words (always, never, only, best)
3. Eliminate options that say something NOT in the paragraph
4. Eliminate options that say the OPPOSITE of the paragraph
5. Between the remaining 2, pick the one that covers the MAIN idea best
